Interest is the payment you earn from the bank just for letting them hold and use your money and is typically calculated as a percentage of the principal(original) amount over a set amount of time. 
When you put money in a savings account or certificate of deposit, the bank uses the deposited funds to lend to other borrowers. Because of this, the bank then pays you interest for allowing them to lend out your money.   
When you borrow money from a bank you have to pay interest on top of the principal amount you borrowed. Banks charge this interest to offset the effects of inflation, make a profit on the funds they are lending out, and because there is chance the borrower might not pay back the loan.
